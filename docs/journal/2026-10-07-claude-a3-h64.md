# 2026-10-07 — Claude — A3 at H 64: results

## What

- The fair H 64 sweep (D-015) finished: 5 policies × 3 budgets × 3 seeds +
  `full` × 3, each evaluated with P1 on the first 100 episodes of the frozen
  test split. 40 new A2 runs; 8 seed-0 runs reused from tuning and the pilot.
- Fairness tuning (val, B-low) chose decay scale 8, relic window 2 (RELIC's
  published 18-frame schedule, D-014), uniform window 1, and 1 sink.
- `scripts/eval/a3_summary.py` pools the seeds and writes
  `docs/results/a3-h64.md` and `docs/results/a3_h64.png`.
- The 256-frame A1 stage also finished (25k steps, val 0.00102).

## Results (LPIPS on revisits 24–31 frames back; lower is better)

| Policy | B-mid | B-low | B-vlow |
|---|---|---|---|
| `window` | 0.091 | 0.157 | 0.194 |
| `window_sink` | 0.094 | 0.172 | 0.215 |
| `uniform_subsample` | 0.136 | 0.174 | 0.203 |
| `relic_discrete` | 0.111 | 0.136 | 0.162 |
| **`decay_continuous`** | **0.084** | **0.100** | **0.130** |
| `full` (oracle) | 0.085 | 0.085 | 0.085 |

- `decay_continuous` is the best budgeted policy at every budget. Against
  tuned RELIC it is significantly better in 19 of 21 budget × condition
  cells; the two exceptions (B-mid 32–47, B-vlow 48–63) are not significant
  either way.
- At B-mid it matches the oracle almost everywhere with a quarter of the
  tokens.
- Its lead also appears on novel views and on revisits beyond the 64-frame
  horizon, where no policy holds the old view. So part of the gain is better
  general context — it keeps more of the recent past in some detail — not
  only better recall of revisited places.

## Caveats

Short range only (H 64, a few seconds). Decay scale 8 and uniform window 1
were at the edge of their tuning grids. One base model, one environment.
The recall-specific part of the gain (revisit advantage beyond the novel-view
advantage) is smaller than the headline numbers and should be reported
separately.

## How to verify

`python scripts/eval/a3_summary.py --runs-root outputs/a3-h64-test
--output-dir outputs/a3-h64-summary` after fetching the eval runs listed in
`docs/a3-h64-jobs.tsv`.

## Plain-language explanation

Across three random seeds and on held-out test mazes, the memory that keeps
every past frame but blurs older ones more was the best of all the
fixed-budget memories, beating RELIC's published scheme at all three budgets,
and at the largest budget it was about as good as perfect memory. Part of the
advantage comes from simply keeping the last few seconds in more detail,
which also helps when nothing is being revisited.
